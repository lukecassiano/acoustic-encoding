        What you are seeing

        The audio is cut into 1.5 s scans and described by 68 features: 60 acoustic (spectrum, timbre, brightness, pitch class, loudness) and 8 musical (tempo, pulse clarity, onset rate, beat regularity, key clarity, mode, roughness, harmonic change). A linear model fitted to one listener's fMRI turns them into a response in every voxel: ŷ(t) = Σd z(x(t − d)) Wd, summed over delays d of 1.5 to 7.5 s, because blood flow trails neural activity by seconds.

        Where it can and cannot see

        Colour appears only where this model predicted held-out music better than chance (clip-permutation test, FDR q < 0.05). That is mostly superior temporal cortex. Hippocampus, cingulate and precuneus carry no measurable music signal in this dataset, which is passive listening to unfamiliar clips, so they stay uncoloured. That gap is a finding, not a rendering fault.

        Surface and slices

        The 3D views place each listener's voxels on a standard surface (fsaverage6), which blurs individual anatomy; glow and spot size are display aids only. The slice view is closer to the data: the listener's own mean functional image from the scan, in native 2 mm voxels, with each voxel's own prediction painted on it.

        How far to trust it, and the dial

        The model learned from 480 fifteen-second GTZAN excerpts (OpenNeuro ds003720); long songs, speech or unusual sounds are out of distribution. Braak stages are tau-PET regions from Pascoal et al. (2020, Brain 143:2818) mapped onto Harvard-Oxford labels; removing them simulates spatial vulnerability only, with no compensation, and is not a model of a patient.
